Paediatric Pain Management

Information for Referring Clinicians

Referral criteria, process, contact details, and clinical reference resources.

Referral Process

Clear and appropriate referrals help us see the right patients at the right time. Please read the criteria below before sending a referral.

The referring hospital specialist should remain involved in the child's care after referral to our service. We work alongside the primary team — we do not take over overall medical responsibility.

Who to refer

Patients should be referred by another hospital specialist involved in the child or young person's care. GP-only referrals are not accepted.

  • Pain has been present for 3 months or more and is affecting function or quality of life.
  • The child or young person has already been assessed or investigated by an experienced clinician, to exclude an underlying, easily treatable cause for their pain.
  • The patient and their family are accepting of the pain diagnosis or pain formulation and not still in the investigation-seeking or diagnosis-seeking phase.
  • The patient is motivated and able to engage with psychology, physiotherapy, pain education and non-pharmacological approaches.
  • Age: 16 years or younger. We will consider 16–18 year olds if they are still under regular review by another RHCYP consultant and still at school.
Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS) is an exception — refer as soon as CRPS is suspected and we will aim to see the patient as soon as possible.

If a young person does not meet the above criteria but you think some advice or guidance from our team would be helpful, please email us and we will be happy to discuss.

How to refer

Please send a referral letter by one of the following routes:

  • Email: loth.rhcyppainteam@nhs.scot
  • Post: Pain Management Clinic, Anaesthetic Department, RHCYP, Edinburgh

The team email is checked by all members of the pain team, which helps avoid delays when individual members are on leave.

Please also refer to the child's local PPALS or CAMHS team at the same time — this minimises waiting time for those valuable services, which we work alongside.

Complex, neurodisabled or palliative patients

For some very complex, cognitively impaired or neurodisabled children and young people, involvement of the palliative care team may be more appropriate than our service. Please get in touch if you would like to discuss.
